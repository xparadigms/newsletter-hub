### 【GoogleがSpaceXに月額9.2億ドルのコンピュート契約】
(구글, SpaceX에 월 9억 2천만 달러 컴퓨팅 계약 체결)
*Google Will Pay SpaceX $920M Per Month for Compute*

▸ What happened

SpaceX has lined up another massive compute deal ahead of its historic IPO, this time with Google. Under the terms, Google will pay SpaceX $920 million per month from October 2026 through June 2029 for access to approximately 110,000 NVIDIA GPUs, CPUs, memory, and related components. The deal mirrors SpaceX's earlier agreement with Anthropic, which pays $1.25 billion per month to rent compute from SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center near Memphis, Tennessee.

SpaceXは歴史的なIPOを前に、Googleとの大型コンピュート契約を締結した。契約条件によると、Googleは2026年10月から2029年6月まで月額9億2,000万ドルを支払い、約11万基のNVIDIA GPU、CPU、メモリなどへのアクセスを得る。この契約は、Anthropicが月額12億5,000万ドルでSpaceXのColossus 1データセンターのリソースを借りる先行契約と類似している。

The AI compute market has entered a hyperscale arms race. Google, despite being the world's largest single owner of AI compute, is struggling to meet surging demand for its Gemini Enterprise agent platform. Alphabet has committed over $180 billion in capex this year and recently announced an $80 billion equity sale to fund further expansion. SpaceX, set to IPO at a $1.75 trillion valuation — the largest in history — is emerging as a key compute infrastructure provider through the data centers originally built for xAI.

With SpaceX's IPO expected to raise $75 billion at a $1.75 trillion valuation, the convergence of space, AI compute, and capital markets is creating entirely new competitive dynamics.

(트럼프 행정부, OpenAI 지분 인수 검토)
*The Trump Administration Might Take an Equity Stake in OpenAI*

President Trump said he has been speaking with AI companies about deals "where the American people can benefit from the success of AI." CNBC reports the Trump administration has been discussing an equity stake in OpenAI specifically, with some of that equity potentially seeding a "Public Wealth Fund" proposed by OpenAI. CEO Sam Altman has reportedly been discussing the idea of government stakes in major AI companies since early 2025.

After a decade on Microsoft's board, LinkedIn co-founder Reid Hoffman is stepping down. Hoffman joined the board after Microsoft acquired LinkedIn for $26.2 billion in 2016. Hoffman says he's going "founder mode" to focus on Manus, a drug discovery startup he co-founded that raised over $50 million in seed funding last year.
